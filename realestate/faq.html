Buying & Investment

Can a foreign national buy property in Japan?

Yes. Japan generally places no citizenship or residency restriction on foreign ownership of freehold land, houses or condominium units. Purchasing property does not, however, automatically grant residency, a visa or permanent residence.

Do I need to live in Japan to complete a purchase?

No. Non-resident buyers may acquire Japanese real estate, although additional documentation and identity verification will be required. A local representative may also be appointed by power of attorney for certain procedures when the buyer cannot attend in person.

Can foreigners obtain a Japanese mortgage?

Financing is possible, but eligibility varies considerably by lender. Japanese banks often consider residency status, permanent residency, domestic income, employment history, Japanese-language ability and the size of the down payment. Non-resident buyers should generally be prepared to purchase in cash or explore specialist international financing.

What additional costs should I budget for when buying?

As a broad guide, acquisition costs often total approximately 6% to 10% of the purchase price. These may include brokerage fees, registration and license tax, judicial scrivener fees, stamp duty, property acquisition tax, loan costs and prorated management or property-tax payments.

What taxes apply after I own the property?

Owners are generally responsible for annual fixed asset tax and city planning tax. Condominium owners also pay monthly management and reserve-fund charges. Rental income and any future capital gain may create additional Japanese tax obligations, so advice from a qualified tax professional is recommended.

What is the difference between freehold and leasehold property?

Freehold ownership includes the building and the associated land interest. Leasehold ownership gives the buyer rights to use land for a defined term under specific conditions. Lease length, renewal provisions, transfer consent and ground rent should be reviewed carefully before purchase.

How long does a typical purchase take?

A straightforward cash purchase may complete in approximately four to eight weeks after an offer is accepted. Mortgage approval, overseas documentation, complex title matters or additional due diligence can extend the schedule.

Can I buy property in Japan as an investment and rent it out?

Yes, subject to building rules, lease regulations and any applicable local restrictions. Investors should evaluate net yield after management fees, reserve contributions, taxes, insurance, vacancy and maintenance rather than relying only on headline rent.

Renting in Japan

Do I need a guarantor (hoshounin) to rent in Japan?

Many landlords do require a personal guarantor or the use of a guarantor company (hoshou gaisha). We specialize in identifying properties that accept guarantor companies, making the process accessible for international tenants who may not have a Japanese guarantor available.

What are key money (reikin) and security deposit (shikikin)?

Shikikin (security deposit) is a refundable deposit, typically one to two months' rent, held against damage or unpaid rent. Reikin (key money) is a non-refundable gratitude payment to the landlord, also usually one to two months' rent. Some modern properties and those marketed to international tenants may waive or reduce reikin.

What documents do I need to rent a property?

Typically you will need a valid passport, residence card (zairyu card), proof of income or employment (such as a contract or pay slips), and sometimes an emergency contact in Japan. Requirements vary by landlord and property, and our team will guide you through the specific documents needed for your application.

How much should I budget for initial move-in costs?

Initial costs in Japan can be substantial. Budget approximately four to six months' rent upfront, which typically covers the first month's rent, security deposit, key money, brokerage fee, guarantor company fee, fire insurance and any applicable lock-change or cleaning fees. We provide a transparent cost breakdown before you commit to any property.

Initial consultations are complimentary and carry no obligation. For completed rental and purchase transactions, standard brokerage fees apply in accordance with Japanese real estate regulations. We explain all costs transparently before any agreement is signed.

What languages does your team support?

Our team provides full professional service in English and Japanese. We also offer support in Mandarin and Spanish through dedicated advisors. All critical contract documents and processes are explained in your preferred language.

Yes. We offer live video viewings via Zoom or WhatsApp for international clients who cannot visit in person. Our agents walk through each property in real time, answering questions and providing context about the neighborhood, building and surroundings.
